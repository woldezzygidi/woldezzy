To grow on social media now, pick one or two platforms that match your goal, post content that earns attention from strangers rather than followers, make it findable in social search, and measure what it does for the business, not just the follower count. Followers matter far less than they did. The feed decides who sees your post, and it decides based on what people watch, not who they follow.

I first wrote this in 2022. The advice then was the standard list: know your audience, post consistently, use hashtags, run some ads. Most of that still holds in a general way, but the mechanics underneath it changed. Recommendation feeds took over every major platform, people started searching inside TikTok and Instagram, and the accounts that grew fastest were often run by a person with a face and a voice, not a brand logo. This is the 2026 version.

Which social media platform should you grow on?

Start with the goal, then pick the platform. Trying to be everywhere is how small teams end up being nowhere. Here is how I match them for clients.

  • LinkedIn for B2B, recruitment and personal authority. Founders and senior people get far more reach from their own profiles than from company pages. LinkedIn has pushed video hard since 2024, and TechCrunch reported video uploads up 36% year on year when it expanded its vertical video tools in early 2025.
  • Instagram for consumer brands, local businesses, visual products and anyone whose buyers already live there. Reels are the discovery surface. The grid is your shop window for people who arrive from elsewhere.
  • TikTok for reaching people who do not know you yet, especially under-35 audiences. It is still the purest interest-graph feed. A new account with no followers can get a large audience on its first good video.
  • YouTube Shorts as the cheap extension of a YouTube channel. Neal Mohan said in June 2025 that Shorts was averaging over 200 billion daily views. Shorts is good for discovery. Long-form YouTube is what builds trust and ranks in Google.
  • X for real-time conversation in tech, finance, politics, sport and media. It still matters in those circles. Outside them, organic reach for brands has become hard to justify.
  • Threads for text-first conversation with an Instagram audience. Meta announced 500 million monthly users in June 2026, which makes it hard to ignore if you are already on Instagram, since the accounts are linked.
  • Bluesky for specific communities, mostly academics, journalists, developers and some creative fields. It is small. Go there if your people are there, not for scale.

My rule for most businesses: one primary platform where you post several times a week, one secondary where you repurpose, and claim your name everywhere else so nobody else does.

How do recommendation feeds decide who sees your posts?

The biggest change since 2022 is the move from the follower graph to the interest graph. In the old model, you posted, your followers saw it, and some of them shared it. In the current model, the platform shows your post to a small test group of people it predicts will be interested, watches how they react, and widens or stops distribution based on that. Followers are one signal among many.

TikTok built this model. Instagram, YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn and Threads all copied it to different degrees. Instagram even made it explicit with Trial Reels in December 2024, which lets you show a Reel only to non-followers first so you can test an idea without it appearing on your grid.

Three things follow from this.

  1. Every post is judged on its own. A big following does not rescue a weak post, and a small following does not stop a strong one.
  2. The first two seconds decide most of it. Watch time, rewatches, saves, shares and comments are the signals the feeds reward. A slow opening kills reach before anything else gets a chance.
  3. Follower count is a lagging indicator. People follow after they have seen several good posts from you. Chasing followers directly, with giveaways or follow-for-follow, tends to bring in people who never engage, which hurts later posts.

Why creator-led and founder-led content grows faster

The accounts that grew fastest over the last few years mostly have something in common: a recognisable person or character at the centre, posting in the native style of the platform, rather than polished brand creative.

For a business this usually means one of two models. Founder-led content, where the founder or a senior person posts from their own account about what they know and what they are building. Or creator-led content, where the brand either has an in-house person who becomes the face, or works with outside creators who already have the audience's trust.

It works because people follow people. The feed rewards content that keeps viewers watching, and a person talking directly to camera, with a clear opinion, holds attention better than a product shot with a caption. It also builds the kind of recognition that carries off-platform. When someone hears your founder on a podcast and then searches your brand name, that is growth, even if no social dashboard records it. I wrote about that gap in the dark funnel post.

The catch is that it depends on a person who is willing to show up regularly and be a bit exposed. If nobody on the team will do that, work with creators instead of forcing it.

Real-world examples of social media growth

I have replaced the 2022 examples. Dove, Airbnb and GoPro were fair examples of brand building, but they were not growth playbooks a smaller business could copy. These three are well documented, and each teaches something specific.

Duolingo on TikTok: a character with a personality

Duolingo turned its owl mascot, Duo, into a chaotic, slightly unhinged TikTok character, playing on the joke that the owl threatens users who skip their lessons. The account was run by a small in-house social team led by Zaria Parvez, who has talked about the approach in interviews since 2022. The content reacts to trends quickly, rarely mentions product features, and treats the comments as part of the show.

In February 2025 Duolingo went further and announced that Duo had died, which Fast Company covered as one of the most talked-about brand stunts of the year. The lesson for a smaller business is not to kill your mascot. It is that a consistent character, with a voice people recognise, gives you something to build every post around.

Ryanair on TikTok: self-aware and fast

Ryanair's TikTok uses a plane with a face added in editing and leans into the jokes people already make about budget airlines. Michael Corcoran, Ryanair's head of social, told Marketing Beat in 2023 that the team is "completely unhinged" on TikTok and that "the more imperfect the content... the better". He described a team of eight, split between an always-on group producing planned content and a react and community group handling news-led posts and comments.

The useful part is that structure. Ryanair separates planned content from reactive content and staffs both. Most brands only plan, which means they miss the trends that move fastest.

Gymshark: creators before it was fashionable

Gymshark started in 2012 near Birmingham, not far from where I am based, and is now headquartered in Solihull. Its founder Ben Francis has said he sent free gear to the fitness YouTubers he admired, and Forbes reported that this early creator seeding drove the brand's first big jump in sales. General Atlantic valued the business at $1.45 billion in 2020.

Gymshark is the creator-led model done early and done properly. It chose creators whose audience matched its customer, kept working with them over years rather than one-off posts, and let them make content in their own style. WARC's write-up of its 66 Days campaign, its first big TikTok push, shows the same approach carried into a new platform.

How to show up in social search on TikTok and Instagram

People search inside social apps now, especially for recipes, products, places, how-tos and reviews. The scale is easy to overstate. An Adobe Express survey covered by Search Engine Journal in February 2026 found most Gen Z respondents had used TikTok as a search engine, but the share who preferred it over Google had fallen. Social search is an additional place people look, not a replacement for Google.

Social posts also show up in Google itself. Since July 2025, public posts from Instagram professional accounts can be indexed by search engines by default. TikTok videos and YouTube Shorts already appeared in Google results. That means your captions and on-screen text are doing SEO work whether you plan for it or not.

What to do about it:

  1. Put the search phrase in the first line of the caption and say it out loud in the video. Platforms read captions, on-screen text and auto-generated subtitles.
  2. Use a profile name and bio that describe what you do in the words customers use, not just your brand name.
  3. Look at the search suggestions inside TikTok and Instagram when you type your topic. They are a free keyword tool for that platform.
  4. Use a small number of specific hashtags. They help categorise a post. They do not create reach on their own any more.
  5. Answer one question per video. A clear answer to a specific question gets found. A general brand video does not.

How to grow on social media: a weekly routine

This is the routine I give small teams. It fits into a few hours a week once it is set up.

  1. Pick your primary platform and one goal. Leads, sales, hiring, awareness. Write it down.
  2. Collect questions. Pull them from sales calls, support emails, comments, Search Console queries and the platform's own search suggestions.
  3. Make three to five short pieces a week, each answering one question or making one point. Open with the point. Cut anything slow.
  4. Keep one slot for reactive content. A trend, a news item, a comment worth replying to with a video.
  5. Reply to comments in the first hour. Early replies keep the thread going, which feeds the signals the platform watches.
  6. Repurpose the winners. A post that did well on your primary platform goes to the secondary one, reworked for its format.
  7. Review once a week. What got watched through, saved and shared. Do more of that.

Consistency still matters, as the 2022 version of this post said, but the reason has changed. Posting often gives the feed more chances to find an audience for you. It is a volume of tests, not a way to stay in front of followers.

How to measure social media growth

Follower count is the easiest number to see and one of the least useful. Here is what I track instead.

  • Reach from non-followers. Most platforms now split this out. It tells you if the feed is distributing your content to new people.
  • Average watch time and completion rate for video. This is the quality signal the feeds care about most.
  • Saves and shares per thousand views. Saves suggest usefulness. Shares, especially into DMs, suggest the content travels.
  • Profile visits and link clicks. The step between watching and acting.
  • Branded search. Watch impressions for your brand name in Google Search Console. If social is working, more people search for you by name.
  • Self-reported attribution. Add a "How did you hear about us?" field to forms and checkouts. It catches what analytics misses.

Expect most of the effect to be invisible to click-based analytics. People see you on TikTok, remember you, and come back weeks later through Google or a direct visit. If you only judge social on last-click conversions, you will cut the channel that was feeding the others. The community post covers what to do once people start turning up.

Quick answers

How long does it take to grow on social media?
Expect three months of regular posting before you can judge a platform fairly. Single posts can take off sooner, but a repeatable pattern takes time to find.

Do hashtags still help?
A few specific ones help platforms categorise a post. They will not create reach for weak content, and stuffing twenty of them does nothing useful.

Should I pay for ads to grow followers?
Rarely. Paid follower campaigns tend to bring people who do not engage. Put ad money behind posts that already proved they work organically, aimed at a business outcome.

Is it better to post from a brand account or a personal one?
For B2B and service businesses, a founder's personal account usually grows faster on LinkedIn. For consumer brands, a brand account with a clear character can work, as Duolingo and Ryanair show.

Pick one platform this week, write down ten questions your customers ask, and turn the first three into short videos that open with the answer. That is the whole strategy, repeated.
