You build an organic presence for free by showing up, accurately and consistently, everywhere a customer or a machine might look for you: Google, Bing and Apple Maps listings, the directories your industry trusts, LinkedIn, the communities where your buyers ask questions, YouTube, and the press. Your website is the hub. The presence is everything that points back to it and agrees with it.

I wrote the first version of this in early 2023. Most of it was about on-page SEO, and it told you to claim your "Google My Business" listing, a name Google dropped in 2021. Since then the listing tools have changed, Apple has rebuilt its business platform, and AI assistants now form opinions about your brand from the whole web. If you want the website side of things, read my beginner's guide to basic SEO, and for content that pulls in visitors, my playbook on free organic traffic. This post is about everything else.

What does "organic presence" mean now?

When a founder asks me how to be found, they usually mean Google rankings. But the people who buy from you find you in more places than that. They see you in the map pack. They ask ChatGPT for a recommendation. They search your name after a colleague mentions you, and judge you by what comes up. They read a Reddit thread before they trust a review site.

Organic presence is the sum of those touchpoints that you do not pay for. Two things make it work. The first is coverage: being present where your customers look. The second is consistency: every place describing you the same way, with the same name, address, phone number, services and story. Search engines and AI systems cross-check sources. When they agree, you become a recognised entity. When they disagree, you become a guess.

How to set up your free business listings

If you serve customers in a place, these three listings come before anything else. They are free, they feed maps and local results directly, and they are increasingly read by AI assistants.

Google Business Profile

Claim or create your profile at business.google.com. Fill in every field that applies: primary and secondary categories, service areas, hours, holiday hours, services, products, attributes, photos and a description written for customers, not keyword lists.

Be aware of what has gone. Google shut down the free websites built through Business Profiles in March 2024, ended the chat feature in July 2024, and from December 2025 began replacing the public Questions and Answers section in Maps with an AI "Ask" feature that answers from your profile details and reviews. That last change matters. The AI can only answer well from what you have published, so complete services, attributes and an accurate description now do the job that seeded Q&A used to do.

Reviews remain the strongest thing you can influence. Ask every happy customer, make it easy with your review link, and reply to every review, good and bad, like a person.

Bing Places

In October 2025 Microsoft relaunched Bing Places for Business and moved it to bing.com/forbusiness. You can import your details from Google, which takes minutes. Do it. Bing's data feeds Bing Maps and Microsoft's own products, and I would rather a client be accurate there than leave it to chance.

Apple Business

Apple Business Connect no longer exists as a separate service. In March 2026 Apple announced Apple Business, which brings Business Connect, Business Manager and Business Essentials into one platform, and it launched in April 2026. You manage your Apple Maps place card at business.apple.com, and getting started is free. Every iPhone user who asks Siri or Maps for a nearby business sees this data, so it is worth an hour of your time.

Which directories and citations are worth your time?

A citation is any mention of your business name, address and phone number on another site. Citations helped local rankings more ten years ago than they do now, but they still do two useful things: they confirm your details to search engines, and some of them send real customers.

Skip the long lists of generic directories. Focus on:

  • Directories your customers actually use in your sector, such as trade bodies, accreditation schemes and professional registers.
  • Your local chamber of commerce and business networks.
  • Review platforms that rank for your category and town.
  • Supplier and partner pages where you can be listed as a stockist, client or member.

Keep a simple spreadsheet of every listing with its login. When you move premises or change your number, update them all in one sitting. Inconsistent details across old listings are one of the most common local problems I fix.

How to use LinkedIn and communities without wasting time

For B2B founders, LinkedIn is the free channel with the highest return I see. Not company pages, which few people follow, but the founder's personal profile and the profiles of the people who do the work.

Post what you know from doing the job: a lesson from a project, a mistake, a short breakdown of how something works. Comment usefully on posts by people your buyers follow. Keep your profile headline and About section consistent with how your website describes the business, because both are read by search engines and AI systems as evidence of who you are.

The same principle applies to Slack groups, Discord servers, industry forums and local Facebook groups. Answer questions. Be the person who knows. Link to your site only when it genuinely answers the question better than a reply could.

How to use Reddit honestly

Reddit has become one of the most visible sources in Google results and in AI answers, and marketers have noticed. That has produced a wave of fake "I tried this tool and loved it" posts. Do not do it. Moderators and users spot it quickly, accounts get banned, and a thread exposing your astroturfing will rank for your brand name for years.

What works:

  1. Use a real account under your own name or clearly tied to your business. Disclose your role when you mention your product.
  2. Read each subreddit's rules before posting. Many ban self-promotion outright.
  3. Spend weeks answering questions in your field with no links at all. Build a history.
  4. When a thread asks for exactly what you offer, say who you are and give an honest answer, including when a competitor would suit them better.
  5. Search Reddit for your brand and category monthly. Reply to complaints in public, calmly.

Done this way, Reddit is slow. It is also durable, because honest answers keep getting found long after you write them.

Is YouTube worth it for a small business?

Usually, yes, if your customers have questions that are easier to show than tell. YouTube is a search engine in its own right, and videos appear in Google results and AI Overviews for how-to and comparison queries.

You do not need a studio. A phone, decent sound and a clear answer to one question per video is enough. Title each video the way people search ("How to bleed a radiator without spilling water"), write a real description, add chapters, and link the video from the matching page on your site. Ten useful videos answering your customers' most common questions will do more than a polished brand film.

How to earn press mentions and links without a PR budget

Digital PR is how small businesses earn the links and mentions that tell Google and AI assistants they are real and respected. You can do a lot of it for free.

  • Answer journalist requests. Follow request hashtags such as #journorequest on social platforms and reply fast with a short, quotable, expert answer.
  • Publish something only you can. Anonymised data from your own work, a survey of your customers, prices you have tracked. Journalists cite original numbers.
  • Pitch local and trade press. They need stories, and a new hire, a milestone or a strong opinion on a local issue can be one.
  • Speak and appear. Podcasts, meetups and webinars nearly always link to their guests.

Do not pay for "guaranteed" links on sites that sell them. Google's spam policies treat paid links meant to pass ranking value as link spam, and the risk lands on your site, not the seller's.

What are entities, and does a small business need one?

An entity is a thing search engines and AI models recognise as distinct and real: a person, a company, a product. Once you are an entity, Google can show a knowledge panel for you and AI assistants can describe you with confidence.

The basics are free:

  • Use exactly the same business name everywhere.
  • Add Organization or LocalBusiness structured data to your site, with sameAs links to your official profiles.
  • Write a short, factual About page that states who you are, what you do, where and since when.
  • If your business meets Wikidata's notability criteria, typically meaning you can be described using serious, publicly available independent sources, create or correct an item. If you do not meet them, do not force it. Earn the coverage first.

This is the part of organic presence that matters most for AI search, and I go into it properly in entities, not keywords.

How do you know it is working?

Brand presence is hard to measure through last-click analytics, because much of its effect shows up as people searching your name later. Watch these:

  • Branded search impressions and clicks in Search Console. Use the branded queries filter if your property has it, or a query filter on your name.
  • Views, calls and direction requests in your Business Profile performance data.
  • How customers say they heard of you. Ask on every enquiry form. It is the cheapest attribution you will ever get.
  • What ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Mode say when you ask them about your business and your category.

Expect your traffic charts to understate all of this. I explain why in the dark funnel.

If you have a free week, spend day one on Google Business Profile, day two on Bing Places and Apple Business, day three fixing your citations, and the rest of the week answering questions where your customers already gather. Then keep going for a year. Free presence is cheap in money and expensive in consistency, and consistency is what most of your competitors will not manage.
