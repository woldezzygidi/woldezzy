§ Abstract

The full picture, in brief.

This independent research examines the digital transformation of the UK FMCG supply chain through the lens of a digital growth strategist. As technologies such as AI, blockchain, IoT, and warehouse robotics gain traction, their impact extends beyond logistics to marketing strategy, customer experience, and ESG reporting.

Drawing on public industry data and UK-based case studies from Tesco, Ocado and The Hut Group, this paper analyses both the gains and losses across stakeholders, including large FMCG firms, SMEs, logistics workers, and consumers. The study outlines how supply chain digitisation enhances traceability, agility, and brand equity while highlighting ethical risks related to workforce displacement and SME exclusion.

The paper concludes with strategic recommendations for growth-focused firms aiming to adopt smart supply chain systems responsibly, ensuring commercial viability and social inclusion in the digital economy.

Research questions

  1. How are digital technologies reshaping operational performance, brand positioning, and customer experience in the FMCG supply chain?
  2. Which stakeholders stand to gain the most from this shift, and which are most at risk of disruption?
  3. What are the strategic, ethical, and reputational implications of this transition, particularly in relation to workforce dynamics, SME inclusion, and data security?
§ Key findings

What the evidence shows.

01

260,000 weekly orders, minimal human intervention

Ocado processes over a quarter of a million weekly orders through its automated fulfilment platform, using machine learning trained on real-time consumer behaviour.

02

40% labour cost reduction at THG

The Hut Group reports a 40 per cent reduction in labour costs since deploying AutoStore robotics in Manchester, with 95 per cent picking accuracy across over 1.2 million SKUs daily.

03

£3bn programme abandoned

Sainsbury's cancelled a £3 billion warehouse automation initiative in 2023 with a £39 million write-off. Scale alone does not buy transformation success.

04

40,000 UK logistics roles at risk

The British Retail Consortium estimates up to 40,000 UK logistics and warehouse roles are at risk over the next five years as automation accelerates.

05

11.9% CAGR to 2033

IMARC projects the UK supply chain technology market to reach $4.6 billion by 2033, confirming supply chain capability as a core pillar of digital transformation strategy.
