Paying for SEO Tools in 2026: Smart Investment or Monthly Money Drain?

By 'Wole Oduwole

Paid SEO tools are worth it when you have a plan, someone with the skill to use them, and a job the free tools cannot do. Without those three, they are a monthly debit alert with a dashboard attached. Most small businesses and new sites can get very far on Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, GA4 and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools, all free, before a paid subscription earns its keep.

I wrote the first version of this in June 2024, and my view has not changed. What has changed is the price list and the menu. The big suites have restructured their plans, almost every vendor now sells some form of AI visibility tracking on top, and the free tools from Google and Bing have started reporting on AI search too. Here is the current picture, as of November 2026.

Why most people overpay for SEO tools

Back in the day, Google Keyword Planner was enough for most small businesses. You typed in a few words, got some search volumes, and you had a content plan. Then came the webinars, the YouTube ads and the threads, making it look like you could not rank a blog post without a $200 a month tool.

The tools are good. That was never the problem. The problem is that most people paying for them use a small fraction of what they pay for. You log in, run a keyword search, stare at the numbers, and close the tab. No strategy, no execution.

I have been there. Paid for the shiny tool, used it for two weeks, then forgot it was active. The debit kept coming every month like clockwork. And you start justifying it: "At least I have it if I need it." I have watched a fashion blogger pay for a suite for half a year and publish three posts. I have watched a freelance writer keep paying because she "didn't want to lose her data", when the data was a backlink report for her own site that the free tools would have given her anyway. That pattern has not gone away. If anything, AI add-ons have made the bills bigger.

What do the main SEO tools cost in 2026?

These are the prices I checked on vendor pricing pages and vendor help pages in November 2026. Ahrefs, Semrush and SE Ranking show US dollars, so the figures below are USD, before VAT. Prices change often, so check the vendor page on the day you buy.

Ahrefs

According to Ahrefs' own pricing guide, the plans are Starter at $29 a month (monthly billing only, with a small monthly credit allowance), Lite at $129, Standard at $249, Advanced at $449, and Enterprise from $1,499 a month. Annual billing on the paid tiers above Starter gives you two months free. Lite is where the full toolset starts. Starter is a taster with tight limits.

The add-on to watch is Brand Radar, Ahrefs' AI visibility product, which starts at $199 a month per AI platform. Custom prompt tracking is sold separately on top of that.

Semrush

Semrush has split its offer in two. On its SEO and AI search pricing page, the SEO-only plan is $139 a month. Semrush One, launched in October 2025, bundles SEO with AI visibility tracking: Starter at $199 a month, Pro+ at $299, and Advanced on custom pricing. Annual billing brings those down. The standalone AI Visibility Toolkit is $99 a month and tracks prompts in ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode. There is also a limited free account.

Moz Pro

I could not load Moz's pricing page to verify the current numbers directly, so I will describe it instead of quoting a figure. Moz Pro runs four self-serve tiers (Starter, Standard, Medium and Large) plus Enterprise. Its Starter plan is the cheapest entry point of the three big suites, with tight limits on tracked keywords and sites. In May 2026 Moz added an AI Visibility dashboard that tracks brand mentions in ChatGPT and Gemini, and it is packaged inside the plan limits rather than sold as a separate add-on. If Moz's link data and Domain Authority are already part of how you report, it is the cheapest suite to stay on.

SE Ranking

SE Ranking's FAQ page lists Core at $129 a month and Growth at $279 a month, with 20% off for annual billing ($103.20 and $223.20 a month), plus a 14-day free trial. Its AI search tracking now sits in a separate product, SE Visible. For freelancers and small agencies who mostly need rank tracking, audits and client reports, it is the best value of the paid suites I use.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider

The Screaming Frog SEO Spider is free for crawls up to 500 URLs. The paid licence removes the limit and adds things like JavaScript rendering, scheduling and integrations with Search Console and GA4. It is £199 a year per user in sterling, and the pricing page currently defaults to €245 a year. It is the cheapest serious technical SEO tool there is, and for site audits I would buy it before any suite.

What can you do with free SEO tools?

More than most people think. This set covers the core of SEO for most sites under a few thousand pages.

  • Google Search Console. Your real queries, clicks, impressions and positions in Google, plus indexing and page experience reports. AI Mode data is counted in the main Performance report, and in June 2026 Google started rolling out separate generative AI reports showing impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode by page. Not every property has those yet.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools. Bing's equivalent, with keyword research and a site scanner. Since February 2026 it has had an AI Performance report in public preview, showing how often Copilot and Bing's AI answers cite your pages and the grounding queries behind those citations. It is free first-party AI citation data, and few people look at it.
  • Google Analytics 4. What visitors do once they arrive, and which pages lead to key events. It is the only one of these tools that tells you whether SEO traffic turns into enquiries or sales.
  • PageSpeed Insights. Core Web Vitals from real Chrome users where Google has enough data, and lab diagnostics for every URL.
  • Ahrefs Webmaster Tools. Free Site Audit and Site Explorer for sites you have verified. Ahrefs' help centre says you get 5,000 crawl credits per project a month and can view up to 1,000 backlinks and keywords for your own site.

Add the free version of Screaming Frog and Google Keyword Planner, and you have audits, backlinks, your own keyword data, technical checks and analytics for nothing. I covered how to use most of this in the basics guide.

What the free stack does not give you is data on other people's sites. Competitor keywords, competitor backlinks and keyword ideas you do not already rank for are what you are paying for when you buy a suite. If you are not doing competitor research every week, you probably do not need to pay for it every month.

Are AI visibility tracking add-ons worth paying for?

Every vendor now wants to sell you a number for how often ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or AI Overviews mention your brand. Ahrefs charges per AI platform, Semrush has both a toolkit and a bundle, Moz folds it into plan limits, and SE Ranking has spun it into a separate product. On top of that are dozens of standalone trackers.

My view, from running these for clients at SEOGidi: they are useful for some businesses and premature for most.

  • The data is a sample. These tools run a set of prompts you choose, a set number of times, and report what came back. AI answers vary between runs, users and locations. Treat the output as a trend line, not a ranking.
  • The prompts are the strategy. If you track the wrong prompts, you get precise data about questions your buyers never ask. Picking prompts well takes the same thinking as keyword research.
  • Each platform reads a different web. Being visible in ChatGPT and invisible in Perplexity is normal. I explained why in the fault lines post. Paying per platform adds up quickly.

Pay for AI visibility tracking when your category is already being discussed in AI answers, you have done the groundwork on your entity and content, and someone will act on the numbers each month. Before that, check Bing's free AI Performance report, look at Search Console's AI data if you have it, and run your key questions through the assistants by hand once a month and write down what you see.

Which SEO tools should you pay for at each business stage?

This is the framework I use with clients. Move up only when the stage below is running properly.

  1. Just starting, under a year old or no rankings yet. Free stack only: Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, GA4, PageSpeed Insights, Ahrefs Webmaster Tools, Screaming Frog free. Spend the money on content and fixing the site. The free organic presence guide covers this stage.
  2. Getting traction, ranking for some terms and publishing regularly. Add a Screaming Frog licence. If you need competitor and keyword data, take one entry-level suite for a month or two when you are planning content, then cancel. Ahrefs Starter, Moz Starter or SE Ranking Core all work here.
  3. SEO is a real channel with a budget and an owner. One mid-tier suite on annual billing, chosen by what your team will open every day. Semrush or Ahrefs if you do a lot of competitor and content research. SE Ranking if reporting and rank tracking matter most. Keep Screaming Frog.
  4. Agency or in-house team running several sites. A higher tier with the seats and projects you need, Screaming Frog for every technical person, and an AI visibility add-on for the clients or brands where AI answers already influence buying. Review usage every quarter and cut seats nobody uses.

Notice what is missing. Nobody needs two overlapping suites. I rarely see a business that genuinely uses Ahrefs and Semrush at the same time, and I see plenty paying for both.

How to get value from a paid SEO tool

If you do pay, make the money work.

  1. Write down the three jobs the tool is for before you buy. Competitor gap analysis, rank tracking, link prospecting, whatever it is.
  2. Start on monthly billing. Move to annual only after three months of regular use.
  3. Set a calendar reminder a week before each renewal and ask: did I use this weekly?
  4. Connect it to Search Console and GA4 where it allows, so it works from your real data, not only its estimates.
  5. Remember that traffic and keyword volumes in third-party tools are modelled estimates. For your own site, Search Console is the truth.
  6. Check whether the add-ons you were sold are being used. AI tracking, content tools and extra projects are where bills quietly grow.

The "before you pay" checklist

The 2024 checklist still holds. I have tightened it.

  • Do you have a written SEO plan? If no, close that pricing page.
  • Have you used the free tools properly for three months? If no, start there.
  • Is there a job only a paid tool can do, such as competitor research or tracking at scale? If no, you do not need it yet.
  • Will someone use it at least three times a week? If no, you are about to donate money.
  • Can you afford to lose that money every month without it hurting? If no, don't swipe the card.

If you cannot tick all five, my brother, my sister, hold your money. If you can, buy one tool, use it hard, and cancel anything you have not opened in a month. If you would rather have someone else do the tool work, that is part of what we do through my services.
